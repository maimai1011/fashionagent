# Fashion Styling

Build a coherent outfit rather than a list of individually attractive pieces.
Treat explicit exclusions, protected pieces, dress codes, mobility needs, and
climate information as hard constraints. Preserve unaffected pieces during a
revision and retain their stable IDs.

Use a restrained palette: normally one or two neutrals plus one accent. Balance
visual weight and silhouette across top, bottom, outer layer, and footwear.
Coordinate material formality and seasonal weight. Accessories should support
the outfit rather than compete with it.

## Color matching

- Neutrals—black, white, cream, grey, navy, camel, olive, and denim—anchor a look.
- Monochromatic palettes use value and texture variation so the outfit does not look flat.
- Analogous palettes combine neighboring hues; keep one dominant and the others supporting.
- Complementary hues create contrast; mute or limit one hue to an accent for everyday wear.
- Repeat an accent once in a small accessory when cohesion benefits, but avoid exact matching everywhere.
- Warm/cool mixing is valid when saturation or value provides a shared visual relationship.
- Consider contrast against the wearer's requested coloring only when that information is actually supplied.

## Outfit composition

- Balance volume: pair an oversized top with a straighter or defined bottom, or a wide bottom with a fitted/structured top.
- Use thirds through tucking, cropped layers, waist definition, or jacket length; do not force this when comfort or modesty conflicts.
- Layers should progress from light/close to heavier/roomier and remain wearable when the outer layer is removed.
- Coordinate formality: polished leather, fine knits, tailoring, and minimal hardware read formal; distressed denim and athletic soles read casual.
- Select footwear for occasion, walking needs, weather, and hem length before decorative accessories.
- Keep accessories purposeful. Usually one focal accessory plus quiet supporting pieces is enough.
- Avoid simultaneous competing statements in silhouette, print, color, and accessories unless maximalism is requested.

## Occasion guidelines

- Work: respect the stated workplace dress code; favor clean lines, practical movement, and layers for indoor temperature changes.
- Formal events: prioritize refined fabrication, intentional shoes, controlled color, and dress-code compliance.
- Smart casual: combine one structured element with relaxed pieces; avoid making every component either highly formal or purely athletic.
- Dates and social events: preserve the wearer's identity and comfort; use one deliberate focal feature.
- Travel: prefer breathable layers, repeatable colors, secure storage, and shoes suitable for the actual walking requirement.
- Outdoor occasions: supplied weather takes priority. Never infer current weather without data.

## Constraints and revisions

- Treat avoid/exclude statements, maximum price, required categories, modesty, allergies, uniform rules, and protected items as hard constraints.
- Never claim weather, body shape, skin tone, size, or gender that was not supplied.
- On revision, identify the smallest affected set. Copy protected and unrelated items exactly, including IDs and attributes.
- If changing one item disrupts palette or formality, adjust only directly dependent details and explain why.
- Do not combine materials that fight the use case: fragile finishes in heavy rain, heavy insulation in heat, or restrictive footwear for long walking.
- If essential information has multiple materially different outcomes, ask the coordinator to clarify rather than silently choose.
